# Portfolio design principles

## Visual first, details on demand

The portfolio should communicate the most important facts before a visitor starts reading paragraphs. Use composition, scale, position, colour, imagery and meaningful motion to make scope, progression and relationships visible at a glance.

Long-form text is the second layer. Keep it available through expandable sections, project pages and focused detail views, but do not make it the only way to understand experience or capability.

Visual presentation must preserve factual accuracy. Timelines use real dates and proportions, company colours remain paired with labels or logos, and no meaning depends on colour or animation alone. All interactions must remain operable with a keyboard, retain visible focus, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Every visual device should serve the portfolio narrative. Avoid decoration that repeats information, creates a false hierarchy between projects or makes the interface harder to scan.
